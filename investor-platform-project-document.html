Section 01

Project Overview

The client is a newly formed real estate investment LLC with a long-term goal of transitioning into a Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT). The business model involves identifying specific investment properties, then gathering a pool of known investors to fund each acquisition — operating as a property management and investment group rather than a broad public fund.

Blackbox Logic has been engaged to design and build two interconnected products: a public-facing marketing website that communicates the company's mission, team, and general property portfolio; and a private, invitation-only investor portal where approved investors can view their positions, fund new opportunities, and receive distributions.

⚡ Immediate Priority

A production-ready public website must be live by end of this week. The investor portal backend will be built in subsequent phases. The portal codebase (currently in demo/mock-data state) will remain in the codebase but be feature-flagged off for the initial launch.

Client Structure

The client is structured as an LLC management group, not a REIT or registered fund. They identify specific properties, then invite known contacts to co-invest in each acquisition. This is a deal-by-deal model rather than a pooled fund, and investor access is invitation-only through pre-existing relationships — which aligns with SEC Regulation D Rule 506(b) private placement exemptions.

Section 02

Regulatory Context

⚠️ Important — Please Read

The client's structure — regardless of LLC formation — constitutes a securities offering under federal law. The LLC wrapper does not exempt them from SEC rules. What they are conducting is a private placement, most likely under Regulation D, Rule 506(b). This is not a loophole; it is a legal framework with specific requirements. A qualified securities attorney must review their structure before any investor funds are accepted.

Rule 506(b) — What It Allows

Under Regulation D Rule 506(b), the client may raise an unlimited amount of capital with the following conditions:

  • Up to 35 non-accredited but "sophisticated" investors may participate alongside unlimited accredited investors
  • No general solicitation or broad public advertising of specific offerings is permitted
  • Raises must be conducted through pre-existing, substantive relationships with investors
  • Detailed disclosures (similar to public offerings) are required if any non-accredited investors are included
  • A Form D must be filed with the SEC within 15 days of the first sale
  • A Private Placement Memorandum (PPM) is strongly recommended

How the Portal Architecture Supports 506(b)

The invitation-only, password-protected investor portal is not just a UX decision — it is part of the client's legal compliance posture. A closed, password-protected portal for pre-existing investors is explicitly acceptable under 506(b). The invite-link model (approved contacts only receive a secure, single-use onboarding link) creates a paper trail of the pre-existing relationship requirement.

📋 Required Client Actions (Before Accepting Funds)

The client must engage a securities attorney to confirm their 506(b) structure, draft a PPM, prepare investor disclosures, and establish a filing process for Form D with the SEC after each offering's first close. State-level "Blue Sky" securities laws in Florida may also apply.

Accredited Investor Verification — Backburner

Under 506(b), formal accreditation verification services (such as Parallel Markets or iCapital) are not required, since the raise is relationship-driven and may include up to 35 sophisticated non-accredited investors. These services have been noted for future use when/if the client transitions to a Rule 506(c) offering or REIT structure, which permits general solicitation but requires verified accreditation for all investors.

Section 06

Current Publishing Plan

The client requires a production-ready public website live by end of this week. The investor portal backend is not yet built, but the demo portal codebase must be preserved for future phases.

Feature Flag Strategy

The cleanest approach is to keep the portal code intact and gate it with a single environment variable. Nothing is deleted or dismantled. When the backend is ready, the flag is flipped and the portal reconnects.

Implementation

Add NEXT_PUBLIC_PORTAL_ENABLED=false to the environment. Any route under /portal or /dashboard checks this flag and redirects to a "Investor Portal — Coming Soon" page with the interest request form. When backend is ready: flip to true and reconnect.

Portal Provider Option (Alternative Path)

A third-party hosted investor portal (such as InvestNext or SyndicationPro) could be deployed faster than a custom backend build. In this scenario, the public site simply includes an "Investor Login" button linking to the provider's hosted portal. This decouples the portal entirely from the custom build.

The current recommendation is to proceed with the custom build (Path A), using the feature flag approach for launch, since the demo scaffolding is already in place and long-term control and branding is more valuable. Path B (third-party provider) remains an option only if client budget or timeline become critical blockers in Phases 2–3.

Section 07

Key Decisions Log

A record of all architectural and strategic decisions made during planning for future reference.

Payment Processor
Using Dwolla (not Stripe) for all ACH payments and investor distributions. Dwolla handles tokenized money movement and Mass Pay. Stripe is excluded from the stack.
Bank Linking
Plaid Link handles bank account connection and instant verification. The Plaid processor token is passed directly to Dwolla. Raw bank account and routing numbers are never stored in the platform database.
Document Signing
DocuSign API is the selected e-signature provider for all investor agreements. Templates will be built in DocuSign console with merge fields for investor and property data.
Cloud Hosting
AWS selected for hosting due to SOC 2, ISO 27001, and PCI-DSS compliance. This is a non-negotiable for a regulated financial platform. Services: Amplify or Elastic Beanstalk (app), RDS PostgreSQL (database), S3 (documents), CloudWatch (monitoring).
Property Management
Client is already on DoorLoop Premium (~$200/mo) which includes API access. DoorLoop is the confirmed property management data source. No migration to AppFolio or Yardi at this stage.
CMS for Marketing Content
Sanity is the selected headless CMS for all public-facing marketing content. Client team can edit founders, events, market updates, and blog posts without developer involvement.
Accreditation Verification
Parallel Markets / iCapital is on the backburner and excluded from the current build. It will be evaluated when the client transitions to a 506(c) structure or REIT requiring verified accreditation.
Portal Strategy at Launch
The demo portal codebase is preserved. A feature flag (environment variable) hides it at launch. When backend phases are complete, the flag is flipped and the portal goes live. Custom build (Path A) preferred over a white-label third-party provider.
Investor Access Model
Invitation-only access. Only manually approved individuals receive a time-limited, single-use, email-locked invite link. This model supports 506(b) compliance requirements for pre-existing relationships.
Regulatory Structure
Client operates under a Regulation D Rule 506(b) private placement framework. The LLC structure does not exempt them from SEC rules. A securities attorney must be engaged before any investor funds are accepted.
Section 08

Open Items & Things to Consider

These are items not yet finalized, questions that should be answered before development progresses, and considerations that did not come up in planning but are important for a project of this type.

01
Securities Attorney Engagement
The client must retain a qualified securities attorney before any investor money is accepted. They need a PPM (Private Placement Memorandum), investor disclosure documents, a Form D filing process, and confirmation of their 506(b) structure. This is not optional — it is a legal prerequisite. Building the technology without legal structure in place creates liability for both the client and potentially Blackbox Logic.
Legal
02
Brand Identity & Design System
Does the client have an established brand? Logo, color palette, typography, and brand guidelines need to be confirmed before the public website is finalized. If brand identity work is needed, that is a separate deliverable that must be scoped and completed before the site can be finalized for launch.
Design
03
Domain, DNS & SSL Certificate
The production domain name must be confirmed and pointed to AWS before launch. SSL/TLS certificate must be provisioned (AWS Certificate Manager handles this for free). The client should also confirm whether they want www-redirects, and whether any subdomains will be used (e.g., portal.domain.com vs domain.com/portal).
Tech
04
Content Readiness for Launch
The public website needs final copy for every section: hero tagline, mission statement, How It Works steps, team bios (with headshots), and property showcase content. Who is providing this content and is there a copywriter involved? A website cannot launch without real content — placeholder Lorem Ipsum is not appropriate for a financial investment company.
Business
05
Legal Page Authorship
Privacy Policy, Terms of Service, and the Investment Risk Disclaimer are legal documents. They should be reviewed or authored by an attorney — not boilerplate generators. These pages have legal standing and need to accurately reflect the company's data practices, investor rights, and regulatory posture. This is especially true given the securities context.
Legal
06
DocuSign Template Preparation
Three DocuSign templates need to be built before Phase 2 can begin: the Risk Disclosure, the Platform Terms of Service, and the Property Subscription Agreement. The Subscription Agreement template requires merge field planning — what investor and property data needs to be dynamically inserted? These templates may require attorney review before being used to bind investor agreements.
Legal
07
Minimum Investment & Distribution Schedule
The $1,000 minimum investment is confirmed. The distribution schedule (monthly, quarterly, per-deal) has not been defined. This directly affects the Dwolla Mass Pay implementation and the investor dashboard's distribution calendar feature. The business model for each property offering needs to be standardized before the payment flow can be fully implemented.
Business
08
DoorLoop API Access & Data Mapping
DoorLoop's API must be tested against the client's actual account and property data. It needs to be confirmed what specific endpoints are available on their Premium tier, what property fields are exposed (address, financials, occupancy, photos), and whether the data structure maps cleanly to the investor portal's property card and detail page designs.
Tech
09
Tax Handling & K-1 Generation
Investors will receive K-1 tax forms annually (or possibly 1099s, depending on deal structure). Who generates these — the client's accountant, the attorney, or a service? The platform needs to store them, but the document generation process is outside the platform scope and must be accounted for in the client's operational workflow. A CPA familiar with real estate syndication should be involved.
Legal
10
Admin Team Training & Operational Playbook
The client's admin team will need to know how to: approve and invite investors, manage DocuSign templates, upload documents to investor accounts, trigger distribution payouts via Dwolla, create and manage property offerings, and respond to failed payment events. An operational playbook and training session should be scoped as a deliverable alongside the platform.
Business
11
Investor Communication Strategy
Under 506(b), investors must be kept informed — monthly, quarterly, or annual updates are expected by law and by investor expectations. The platform should support admin-authored updates pushed to investors, but a communication cadence and content strategy needs to be defined. Is this handled in-platform, or via a separate email marketing tool like Mailchimp or ConvertKit?
Business
12
REIT Transition Planning
The client's end goal is REIT status. The platform architecture has been designed with this in mind (AWS infrastructure, Yardi as a future property management upgrade, Parallel Markets for accreditation). However, a REIT requires a minimum of 100 shareholders, 75% of assets in real estate, and specific income distribution requirements. This should be tracked and planned with their attorney as the portfolio grows, so the technology transition is not disruptive.
Business
13
Accessibility Compliance (ADA / WCAG 2.1)
Financial platforms are increasingly subject to ADA accessibility requirements under WCAG 2.1 AA standards. This is both a legal consideration and good practice. Key requirements include proper contrast ratios, keyboard navigation, screen reader compatibility, and accessible form labeling. An accessibility audit should be part of the Phase 7 QA process.
Tech
14
Backup, Disaster Recovery & Data Retention Policy
AWS RDS provides automated backups, but a formal disaster recovery plan and data retention policy should be documented. How long are investor records kept? What happens to data if an investor closes their account? For a financial platform holding SSNs, EINs, and bank data, this needs to be answered in writing and reflected in the Privacy Policy.
